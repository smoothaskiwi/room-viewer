What the picture means

One person can pass single file: 23.45 ± 2.1 in, below the 30-inch comfortable guide; with a drawer or the white door open, nobody can pass comfortably. The Miles bed is about 4.3 inches longer than today’s mattress; this and the deeper dresser shrink today’s roughly 36.0-inch walkway.

East-window conflict, both queen layouts: The headboard covers the lower north corner of the east window: 5.6 ± 3.0 in wide × 15.0 ± 3.0 in high. The bed is pulled 2.0 in off the east wall to clear its sill; without this setback it would overlap 9.6 ± 3.0 in of the sill. The north bed edge is unchanged. The setback is conditional on measuring the sill; it is included in every gap here.

A pulled-out drawer leaves 13.45 ± 2.1 in. With the white door open, the reference gap is 13.7 ± 2.1 in. Both are too narrow for the 22-inch person marker.

The dresser overlaps the white door trim by 2.5⁠–⁠3.7 in (survey to photo fit), with combined uncertainty ± 1.8 in. Both estimates say the white door opens to about a right angle and rests against the dresser end (about 93 deg survey, 90.5 deg photo fit), but the margin is thin: the dresser's end would stop only 0.15 to 1 inch short of the door's hinge edge. If it ends up past the hinge edge even half an inch (for example if the north window sill sticks out more than 2 inches), the door would be blocked early, at roughly 15 to 30 degrees. Across the combined hinge/stool uncertainty, the modeled stop is 17.4⁠–⁠98.7°. Not purchase-ready.

The shorter alternative clears trim by 7.5⁠–⁠8.7 in ± 1.8 in, with door stop 122° best; 118.0⁠–⁠125.6° range versus 156° best; 154.4⁠–⁠164.6° range today. It narrows the aisle to 22.15 ± 2.1 in, below the 24-inch tight-passage guide. Its drawer-open gap is 13.45 ± 2.1 in and open-door reference gap 13.7 ± 2.1 in. Every Miles dresser in West Elm's US line is 18 in deep or more (60, 70: 18; 34: 18.3; 48 narrow and 49 wide: 19.3), so no Miles dresser on the west wall widens the aisle.

The dresser’s north end stands 2.0 ± 1.0 in off the wall for the north-window stool. It covers 2.75⁠–⁠4.75 in ± 1.5 in of that window’s lower west trim. The bed stays 26.0 ± 4.0 in from the north wall. The rack location is approximate (about ± 8 in); its apparent roughly 1.5-in clearance to other door swings is not a verified fit.

Five measurements to settle it

  1. On the west wall above the baseboard, measure from the north wall to the hinge edge of the white door, where the door meets its frame on the hinge side (estimates 62.15 to 63 in). Also measure how far the north window's sill (stool) sticks out from the wall (estimate 2 in). If the hinge edge is at least 60 inches plus the sill projection from the north wall, the 60-inch dresser stops short of it and becomes the door's stop at about a right angle; if not, the dresser sits in front of the hinge and blocks the door early.
  2. From the north wall to the outer north edge of the white door's trim (estimates 58.3 to 59.5 in), and how far that trim stands out from the wall compared with the baseboard (if it stands out more, the dresser must sit out from the wall by the difference, narrowing the walkway).
  3. On the east wall: from the north wall to where the east window's sill and trim begin (estimate 81 in), how far that sill sticks out (estimate 2 in), and its height from the floor (estimate 28 to 30 in). The queen's 45-inch headboard reaches 90.6 in from the north wall, so it overlaps that window.
  4. Clear room width from the west wall to the east wall at about 30 inches high, plus baseboard thickness.
  5. White door width, and how far it opens today before hitting anything.

About the door angle

The one-sided 1.375-in slab never passes north of the hinge through 90°. White door: 93° best; 17.4⁠–⁠98.7° range against storage. Photo fit 90.5°; hinge margin 0.15⁠–⁠1.0 in. Open-door gap: 13.7 ± 2.1 in. The range propagates the hinge edge and north-window sill standoff together; installed stop and lever remain unmeasured. An early stop changes the exact door-gap position. Tape checks settle it.

North-window stool standoff: 2.0 ± 1.0 in. Dresser top is above the stool. The 60-inch dresser also covers the west-wall outlet.

Plus or minus bands are estimates, not guaranteed limits. The entry and closet swings are shown through 90°; the closet hinge is a survey assumption. The laundry rack may need moving when either door opens.

Planning guides, not building-code claims

Rules of thumb used in residential furniture planning: about 24 in is a tight single-file squeeze, 30 in is comfortable, 36 in is generous and the usual allowance in front of a dresser for opening drawers and standing. For reference, 36 in is also the US International Residential Code minimum hallway width (IRC R311.6). These are guides, not code requirements for bedrooms. Residential planning comparisons from the room survey, not a mobility-accessibility test.
